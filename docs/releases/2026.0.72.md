# VOCO 2026.0.72

Dictation is the first step. We're building the voice layer for Linux: private,
local, and under your control.

**Today: private dictation for Linux.** Press a shortcut, speak, and your words
appear where you are typing, while you speak.

On GNOME 50, this release has the snap type a dictation's last words sooner,
and keeps the words it types while you hold a modifier key. In the snap you
now change the shortcut in VOCO's own Settings, and Help can open a bug
report with your version and desktop filled in. It runs on the same
systems as 2026.0.71: Ubuntu 24.04 and 26.04, Debian 13 and Fedora 44. The
[changelog](../../CHANGELOG.md) lists every change.

## What's new

- **Your last words arrive sooner in the snap on GNOME 50**, which Ubuntu 26.04
  has. The snap couldn't see your keyboard, so after the press that stops a
  dictation it waited a fixed time to be sure the shortcut's keys were up
  before typing. GNOME now tells VOCO whether Shift, Control, Alt or Super
  is down, and nothing else about your keyboard, so VOCO types as soon as they
  are up. In our test on Ubuntu 26.04, the last words arrived 0.22 to 0.27 s
  after the press, where 2026.0.71 took 0.50 to 0.63 s.
- **Words typed under a held key are no longer lost in the snap on GNOME 50.**
  If you were holding Control, Alt, Shift or Super when VOCO typed, such as
  Super pressed well before X, the words never arrived. VOCO now waits for
  you to let go.

- **The snap's shortcut changes in VOCO's Settings.** Once GNOME held the
  shortcut, VOCO sent you to GNOME Settings to change it. Now open
  **Settings**, then **Shortcut**, choose **Change shortcut**, type the new
  one or choose **Record keys**, and choose **Apply shortcut**. On GNOME 48
  and later GNOME shows the new shortcut in its own dialog: choose **Add**.
  On Ubuntu 24.04 there is no dialog. The presets in the tray's **Change
  shortcut** menu work the same way, and any shortcut with Alt, Ctrl or
  Super and another key GNOME can name can be used.
- **The last letters are no longer lost when you stop as VOCO types.** In the
  snap on GNOME 50, pressing the shortcut in the same instant as VOCO typed
  could drop those letters. VOCO now holds them until your keys are up.

- **The shortcut is quicker in the snap on Ubuntu 24.04.** GNOME 46 started a
  new VOCO process for each press, which took about 150 ms; its shortcut now
  talks to the VOCO that is already running. Recording starts 150 ms sooner,
  and a second press soon after the first is no longer ignored. VOCO updates
  the shortcut it added to GNOME's keyboard shortcuts the first time it
  starts.

- **Quit VOCO lets the Snap Store update the snap.** Small helper programs
  that keep the clipboard used to stay behind after you quit, and the store
  held its update back until you signed out. VOCO now ends them when it
  quits; what you copied last can still be pasted.

- **Report a problem.** Open **Settings**, then **Help**, and choose
  **Report a problem**: your browser opens VOCO's bug form with your version,
  package and desktop filled in. VOCO sends nothing itself. You finish the
  form and decide whether to submit it.
- **On X11, the popover stays open.** Clicking VOCO's launcher sometimes
  showed the popover for an instant and closed it. It now stays until you
  click elsewhere.

On Ubuntu 24.04, GNOME 46 doesn't tell VOCO about the keyboard, so the snap
still waits a fixed time before it types a dictation's last words. The `.deb` and the RPM never typed through GNOME's
portal, so nothing changes in how they type.

## Upgrade

**The snap** updates itself from the Snap Store. If it says an update is
waiting, choose **Quit VOCO** in the tray or panel menu, then open VOCO again.

**From 2026.0.69, 2026.0.70 or 2026.0.71**, open **Settings**, then
**Updates**, and choose **Install update**. Your system asks for an
administrator's password, and VOCO restarts on this release. From 2026.0.66 to 2026.0.68 on Debian 13, Ubuntu 26.04
and Fedora 44, upgrade once with the guided installer below.

From an earlier version:

1. Finish any dictation, and copy anything you need from Review.
2. Quit VOCO with **Quit VOCO** in the tray or GNOME panel menu.
3. Run this release's guided installer, or repeat the
   [manual install](../install.md#manual-install):

   ```bash
   wget -qO voco-install https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sergiopesch/voco/voco.2026.0.72/install && bash voco-install
   ```

Your settings are kept, including your shortcut. The GNOME panel is still
version 23, so an upgrade from 2026.0.70 or 2026.0.71 needs no sign-out. From an earlier
version on GNOME, save your work and sign out and back in after upgrading, so
GNOME loads panel version 23. `voco --check-panel` says when a sign-in is still
needed.

## Known limits

- In the snap, GNOME shows its orange remote-access icon while VOCO types,
  unless you add the VOCO panel. On Wayland desktops other than GNOME the snap
  can't type; use the `.deb` or RPM.
- On Ubuntu 24.04, GNOME 46 tells VOCO nothing about your keyboard unless you
  add the VOCO panel. The snap types a dictation's last words a little under a
  second after you press to stop, and words it types while you hold Super or
  Alt on its own, such as Super pressed well before X, don't arrive.
- GNOME's Settings has no switch for the snap's typing permission. VOCO keeps
  GNOME's answer in its own settings folder; `sudo snap remove --purge voco`
  removes it.
- Install update needs your system's password prompt, which your desktop
  provides. Over SSH, or in a session that isn't the active local one, the
  system refuses it; use the release's installer instead.
- Dictation replaces what the clipboard and the primary selection hold.
- My words makes VOCO lean toward your words but can't guarantee them: one that
  sounds exactly like another, or that VOCO hears as a different word, can still
  come out wrong. **Say it** shows what VOCO hears, and **Teach VOCO** fixes a
  version it gets wrong.
- A taught version made only of everyday words changes those words every time
  you say them.
- Where you pause between sentences, the full stop often comes a word late or
  not at all, with no capital after it. VOCO doesn't add one, because it would
  also end sentences where you only pause to think
  ([the measurements](../../research/pause-sentence-ends-2026-10/README.md)).
- After a pause, a rich-text field can make the next space a no-break space, at
  most one for each pause, and may keep the space Stop adds as one.
- On Wayland, session checks are not an atomic lock: switching sessions after a
  check can still redirect keys. An unavailable logind status permits pasting.
  See the [input-device security model](../security/README.md#input-devices).
- GNOME's own microphone indicator can still shift the top bar's icons while the
  microphone is in use.
- On Debian 13 and Fedora 44, VOCO has an icon in the top bar only once the GNOME
  panel is on, because they don't turn on an AppIndicator extension.
- DNF says it skipped OpenPGP checks for the RPM file. The installer verifies the
  signed checksums first, which is what authenticates the package.
- VOCO discards a recording it can't confirm is complete, and says so at Start.
- When it pastes, VOCO can't tell a password field from other fields. Don't
  dictate into one. The Chromium extension refuses password fields.
- Don't dictate while a terminal program is waiting for an answer, such as a
  coding agent's permission prompt: it may read your words as typed keys. See
  [Where your words go](../everyday-use.md#where-your-words-go).
- VOCO knows the screen is locked only when the desktop reports it to logind, as
  GNOME does.
- Apps that remap Shift+Insert, remote desktops and virtual machines may not
  accept the paste. Custom editors and other compositors need their own testing.
- On Wayland, a shortcut other than Super+X, Alt+D or Alt+Shift+D works only
  when you bind it to `voco --toggle` in your desktop's keyboard settings.
- With Alt+D, apps with a menu bar can still open it when you press the
  shortcut, and lose words; Super+X avoids that.
- In the snap on X11, a microphone permission granted after VOCO started takes
  effect only after a restart; VOCO says so.
- VOCO recognizes English only. It needs x86-64 with AVX2, FMA and F16C, and
  glibc 2.39 or later.
